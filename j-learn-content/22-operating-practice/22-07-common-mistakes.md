# Common Operating Mistakes

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

This section is the consolidated "what not to do" list for everyday amateur operating. Each entry is a mistake that's easy to make, easy to fix, and gets noticed by other operators when it happens.

Read it once and you'll never be the operator everyone gripes about on the cluster.

## Mistakes that QRM others

### Not asking "is the frequency in use?"

Tuning to 14.205, hearing nothing for 5 seconds, calling CQ. There may be a QSO already in progress between two stations you can't hear (skip-zone propagation). Always ask QRL? or "frequency in use?" first.

**Fix**: §22-01. Ask twice with a pause; listen 10 seconds; then call.

### Tuning on an active frequency

Pressing "tune" on the rig while it's set to 14.205 mid-QSO drops a 5-15 second carrier on top of someone's contact. Disastrous.

**Fix**: §22-02. Use a dummy load. If you must tune on the air, listen first, move 3-5 kHz off, announce "tuning, please stand by."

### Calling on the DX's TX frequency

In a split pile-up, transmitting on the DX's TX frequency (instead of their listen range) is the cardinal sin. Universally hated.

**Fix**: §22-05. Read the DX's split announcement; transmit on the listen frequency only.

### Long transmissions

A 4-minute monologue on FM blocks four other operators who could have shared the channel. On HF SSB it ties up a frequency that others might want to use.

**Fix**: §22-04. Make your point in 30-90 seconds, then pass back. The other operator can fill 30 seconds of their own commentary.

## ID mistakes

### Forgetting end-of-QSO ID

"73, thanks for the contact" without your callsign at the end is technically a §97.119 violation.

**Fix**: §22-03. End every QSO with your callsign — even if you've said it three times in the previous transmission.

### Tactical-only ID

In emcomm, using only "Net Control" or "Shelter Bravo" without the FCC callsign within 10 minutes is a violation.

**Fix**: §21-05 + §22-03. Tactical callsigns are fine for the conversation; FCC ID at sign-off and every 10 minutes is required.

### Tuning without ID

Continuous carrier with no callsign before or after is technically a §97.119 violation. Not legal.

**Fix**: §22-02. Brief callsign before and after the tune.

## QSO flow mistakes

### Running over the other operator

Talking before they finish their transmission. On SSB this is just rude; on FM it produces double-tones and unintelligible audio.

**Fix**: Wait for the squelch tail or a clear "over" before transmitting.

### Failure to acknowledge

Ignoring "WM3J, do you have a break?" or "WM3J, please QSY 14.215." If someone calls you, acknowledge — even if just to say "stand by" or "QSL, going to 14.215."

**Fix**: Listen for your callsign; respond promptly.

### Not pacing yourself

Spending 90 seconds discussing your antenna while a contest is running on the same band, or while others are waiting for the frequency.

**Fix**: §22-04. Brevity in proportion to the situation. Casual chat = leisure pace; pile-up or contest = fast.

## Phonetic and copy mistakes

### Mumbled phonetics

"Whiskey Mike Three Juliet" said too fast or too quietly. The receiver can't copy it; they ask you to repeat; you've doubled the time.

**Fix**: Say each phonetic distinctly. Slow down by 20% from your conversational pace.

### Inconsistent phonetics

Saying "WM3J" once as "Whiskey Mike Three Juliet" and once as "Whiskey Mike Three Joliet" (a city name). The receiver may not realize you mean the same callsign.

**Fix**: §19-04. Stick to NATO/ICAO phonetics; rehearse your own callsign.

### Failing to confirm spellings

Receiving station copies "John Smith" but isn't sure if it's "Smith" or "Smyth." They ask. Sender repeats just the name without phonetic spelling. Mystery unsolved.

**Fix**: §21-04. Use I SPELL with phonetics. "Smith. I spell. Sierra Mike India Tango Hotel. Smith."

### Garbled CW

Sending CW too fast for the receiver to copy. They send "QRS PSE" (please slow down). You ignore and send the same thing again at the same speed.

**Fix**: Listen for QRS requests; slow down. Most CW operators copy 12-18 WPM comfortably; only specialists handle 25+ WPM.

## Power mistakes

### Running max power for everything

100 W is enough for most QSOs. Going to 1500 W is rarely justified.

**Fix**: §22-06. Start at moderate power; increase if needed; reduce when conditions improve.

### Using a vertical at 1500 W

A poorly-grounded vertical at 1500 W is a worse signal than a good Yagi at 100 W *and* causes more local QRM.

**Fix**: §17-08. Improve the antenna first; use modest power.

### Not adjusting for conditions

Running 200 W when the band has opened to 1000-mile-deep is excessive. Drop to 50 W when the band is hot.

**Fix**: Stay attentive; reduce when you can.

## DX and pile-up mistakes

### Calling outside your category

DX calls "CQ Europe only" — and someone in the US calls back. Wastes both stations' time.

**Fix**: §22-04. Listen to who the CQ is targeted at; only call if you qualify.

### Repeat-calling in a pile-up

Calling 8 times in a row when not picked. The DX has heard you; they're working someone else.

**Fix**: §22-05. Call once or twice, listen, give others a turn.

### Spotting yourself

Putting a self-spot on the DX cluster is universally embarrassing.

**Fix**: Let other operators decide if your contact is worth spotting.

### Long contests in conversational tone

Trying to chat during a contest exchange. Contesters need: callsign + report + serial number. That's it. "How's the weather?" wastes everyone's score.

**Fix**: Match cadence to the operating style. Casual = chat; contest = exchange-only.

## Equipment and station mistakes

### Hot mic / open mic

Pressing PTT and walking away. Or having a "stuck" key (CW key bouncing in your hand) that broadcasts a continuous carrier.

**Fix**: Always release PTT before moving away from the mic. Check CW key for cleanliness and stability.

### Voice operating with severe RF feedback

The microphone audio is full of buzz, distortion, or echo because RF is getting back into the audio chain. Listeners can't copy you.

**Fix**: §13-04. Choke the coax, ground the mic, isolate the audio. Don't keep transmitting if you can't be copied.

### Transmitting with poor grounding

Symptoms: crackling audio, distortion, occasional smoke, painful "RF tickle" when you touch the rig.

**Fix**: §13-05. Ground the rig at a common point; check the AC mains ground. Don't operate unsafely.

### Cross-band operating without coordination

A repeater on 2 m linked to a repeater on 70 cm with no announcement to either user base. Confuses everyone.

**Fix**: Announce cross-band coordination in advance; all users on both bands should know.

## Net operating mistakes

### Talking through net control

NCS is calling for check-ins; you transmit your own check-in over them.

**Fix**: §21-05. Wait for NCS to finish; then transmit briefly.

### Disappearing from a net

Checking in then leaving without saying so. NCS wastes time calling you for traffic.

**Fix**: §21-05. Always check out: "WM3J requesting permission to secure."

### Long announcements

Spending 30 seconds explaining your situation to NCS during check-in. Other operators are waiting.

**Fix**: Brief check-in; details on the side frequency only when needed.

## Cross-mode mistakes

### "CQ" on a repeater

Saying "CQ DX" on a 2 m FM repeater. Repeater conventions are different.

**Fix**: §22-04. "WM3J listening" on a repeater; "CQ DX" on HF SSB.

### FM SSB-style

Trying to ragchew on a 2 m calling channel (146.52 MHz) where simplex contacts are typically brief, then move to a different frequency.

**Fix**: After the initial contact on a calling channel, QSY to a separate working frequency for the conversation.

### SSB modulation issues

Unbalanced audio, severe ALC clipping, distortion. Listeners ask "your audio is splattered."

**Fix**: §13-03. Set ALC properly (compression to taste; don't slam the limiter); use noise-cancelling mics in noisy shacks; listen to your own monitored audio occasionally.

## Cultural mistakes

### Operating without proper authorization

Using a station you don't have permission to use. Borrowing someone else's callsign without their authorization.

**Fix**: Use your own callsign or your authorized club station. Authorization matters.

### Discussing politics or sensitive topics

§97.111(a) forbids transmitting messages that are "encrypted with hidden meaning or false signals" — and broadly, amateur radio convention frowns on overtly political or commercial discussions on the air.

**Fix**: Talk about radio, weather, equipment, hobbies. Save politics for elsewhere.

### Using callsigns of silent keys

Continuing to use the callsign of a deceased operator (a "silent key"). Not legal; the callsign is retired.

**Fix**: Honor SK with a memorial QSO or operating event, not by using their call.

### Slow phonetics for "fancy" words

Phonetic-spelling words that don't need it. "WM3J listening on 146.94 Whiskey Mike Three Juliet listening one four six point nine four" — overkill.

**Fix**: Phonetic-spell only when conditions or unfamiliarity warrant it. "WM3J" is enough on FM.

## When you've made a mistake

If you've QRMed someone, splattered, gotten the call wrong, or violated some convention:

- **Acknowledge it.** "WM3J, sorry for the QRM, didn't realize you were there."
- **Don't escalate.** A short apology beats a long defense.
- **Learn from it.** Make a note; don't repeat the mistake.

The amateur community is forgiving when operators acknowledge errors and improve. It's harder when errors are repeated despite feedback.
