# Propagation — Overview

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

## What this chapter is for

Radio waves do not travel in straight lines past the horizon. The atmosphere refracts, absorbs, and reflects them in ways that change every hour, every season, every solar cycle. Understanding propagation is what separates "I keep calling CQ but nobody answers" from "I worked Japan on 100 watts on a wire dipole."

This chapter teaches you enough to:

- Look at today's solar numbers and decide which band to call CQ on.
- Predict which paths will be open in three hours, when you sit down at the radio.
- Understand why a station 500 km away is loud and a station 200 km away is silent.
- Recognize the unusual openings — sporadic E, trans-equatorial, greyline — and exploit them.

## What to read first

If you're brand new, start with **01-08 (Band Choice Right Now)** for a quick win, then come back and read **01-01 (Solar Indices)** so the numbers stop being mysterious. Everything else can wait until you actually hit a situation that confuses you.

If you already operate but don't know why, read **01-04 (Ionospheric Layers)** first. It's the mental model everything else hangs off.

## What you will not learn here

- VHF/UHF tropospheric ducting and meteor scatter — those live in the satellite and weak-signal chapters.
- EME (moonbounce) — fascinating, but a different category of physics.
- Detailed antenna patterns — chapter 06 covers those.

## Where to look up the numbers

- **NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center** — `swpc.noaa.gov`
- **hamqsl.com** — Paul Herrman N0NBH's solar dashboard, the most-used in the hobby
- **DR2W solar terminator map** — for greyline times
- **PSK Reporter** (`pskreporter.info`) — see who's actually being heard, right now, on any band

J-Hub pulls SFI, A, and K every few minutes from NOAA SWPC and shows them on the Dashboard tab. You don't usually need to leave the suite to check.
