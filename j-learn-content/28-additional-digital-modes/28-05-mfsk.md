# MFSK16 and MFSK32 — Conversational Multi-Tone FSK

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

## What it is

MFSK16 and MFSK32 are members of the same multi-tone FSK family as Olivia (§28-04), but tuned for **conversational rate** rather than maximum weak-signal copy. Designed by Murray Greenman (ZL1BPU) and Nino Porcino (IZ8BLY) in the early 2000s, they predate Olivia and were among the first popular MFSK modes on amateur HF.

The trade-off compared to Olivia: **faster typing, less robustness at the noise floor**. MFSK16 copies at about 42 WPM (roughly handwriting speed); MFSK32 doubles that to ~84 WPM. Both work well on moderately-stable HF paths where Olivia would feel painfully slow.

MFSK modes are most active on **HF nets** — informal regional groups that meet on schedule. They're a friendly community, often willing to teach new operators the mode in real time.

## How it works

| Property | MFSK16 | MFSK32 |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Number of tones | 16 | 32 |
| Tone spacing | 15.625 Hz | 31.25 Hz |
| Total bandwidth | ~316 Hz | ~630 Hz |
| Symbol rate | 15.625 baud | 31.25 baud |
| FEC | Convolutional rate-1/2, K=7 | Convolutional rate-1/2, K=7 |
| Decoder threshold | ~−13 dB SNR | ~−10 dB SNR |
| Typing speed | 42 WPM | 84 WPM |
| Character coding | Varicode (variable-length) | Varicode |

Both modes spread each character across multiple tones with **convolutional FEC** (rate 1/2 — every information bit is encoded as two transmitted bits with a Viterbi-style decoder on the receive side). The receiver tracks the maximum-likelihood path through a trellis of possible bit sequences.

**Varicode** is similar to PSK31's varicode: common characters (space, lowercase letters) use short codes; rare characters (uppercase, punctuation) use longer codes. This compresses average throughput in normal English text.

> **Advanced —** MFSK's tone constellation is **orthogonal** in the symbol period — each tone is exactly an integer number of cycles long within one symbol interval, so adjacent tones produce zero cross-correlation in an ideal channel. This is what allows narrow tone spacing without inter-symbol interference. Frequency-selective fading on a real ionospheric path breaks orthogonality on some tones at some moments, which is why MFSK has FEC — the FEC reconstructs symbols that lost their orthogonality during deep fades. MFSK32's wider tone spacing makes it more fade-robust than MFSK16 *as a fraction of bandwidth*, but less weak-signal-sensitive in absolute terms.

## Why use it

- **Conversational pace.** Faster than Olivia, slower than RTTY — feels like natural typing.
- **Decent weak-signal margin.** Several dB worse than Olivia, but still 10+ dB better than RTTY.
- **Narrow.** MFSK16 in 316 Hz fits comfortably between other signals on a crowded band.
- **Stable community.** Long-running nets (e.g. the "MFSK Round Table" on 20m) provide reliable on-air contacts.
- **Excellent for emcomm training.** Forgiving of marginal stations and slower than RTTY for new operators to follow.

When to skip it: maximum-DX weak-signal work (use Olivia 8/250 or FT8), contesting (RTTY or FT4 are standard), or any case where the fixed-message efficiency of FT8 wins.

## Operating

**Software:** **Fldigi** is the standard. It supports MFSK4, 8, 11, 16, 22, 31, 32, 64, and 128 — though 16 and 32 are the practically-used versions.

**Calling frequencies (USB dial):**

| Band | MFSK center |
|------|-------------|
| 80m | 3.580 MHz |
| 40m | 7.080 MHz |
| 30m | 10.143 MHz |
| 20m | 14.080 MHz |
| 17m | 18.105 MHz |
| 15m | 21.080 MHz |
| 10m | 28.080 MHz |

Activity is light compared to FT8 — most days you'll find one or two MFSK QSOs going, often via a scheduled net.

**RsID** (Reed-Solomon Identifier) is recommended — same as Olivia. It announces the mode and audio frequency, letting receivers auto-configure to MFSK16 vs. MFSK32 vs. other MFSK variants. Always enable RsID.

**A typical MFSK16 QSO:**

```
TX: CQ CQ CQ DE K1ABC K1ABC K1ABC PSE K
RX: K1ABC DE W2DEF W2DEF
TX: GM ES TNX FOR CALL = UR RST 579 579 = NAME JOHN JOHN
    = QTH BOSTON MA BOSTON MA = RIG IS K3 RUNNING 25W
    = ANTENNA IS DIPOLE AT 30FT = HW CPY? BTU W2DEF DE K1ABC K
```

At MFSK16's rate, this takes about 60 seconds; at MFSK32 about 30 seconds.

**Power level:** 10–50 W typical. The mode's FEC gives it enough margin that QRO power is unnecessary; running over 100 W is considered loud.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| Decoder produces gibberish | Wrong mode (MFSK16 vs MFSK32 vs MFSK64) | Send/receive RsID; or manually try alternates |
| Partial decode | Drift or selective fade | Wait — MFSK FEC catches up next characters |
| Garbled with periodic missing characters | Computer audio dropouts | Increase Fldigi audio buffer size |
| Decoder produces lower-case only / no caps | Stuck on varicode shift | Wait — usually self-recovers next character |
| TX on but ALC pumping | Audio over-driven | Lower PC audio output until ALC barely lights |
| No QSOs found on calling frequency | Mode is quiet — try a net | Check qrz.com nets list for MFSK nets; or skim 14.080 ±500 Hz |
| Sideband-confusion (mark/space inverted) | Rig in LSB instead of USB | Use USB on all HF bands |
| Slow throughput | Stuck on MFSK16 when MFSK32 would work | Manually switch; both stations must agree |

## Common mistakes

- **Picking the slowest variant by default.** MFSK16 is the historical "default" but MFSK32 is just as well-supported and twice as fast. If the path is solid, run MFSK32.
- **Forgetting RsID.** Without it, others can't auto-tune to your config.
- **Operating outside the MFSK frequency band.** This isn't a "calling channel" mode like FT8 — the activity is sparse, so go to the published frequencies.
- **Treating MFSK like Olivia.** Olivia's edge over MFSK is at very weak signals; on a clean path MFSK is faster and equally easy. Pick the right tool for the conditions.
