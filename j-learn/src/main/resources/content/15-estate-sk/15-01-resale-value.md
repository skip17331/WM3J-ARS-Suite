# Resale Value & Where to Sell

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> ⚙️ **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

This section is for **you**, the operator, planning the document. It covers how to estimate the current market value of your equipment and which channels exist for selling it. You'll use this information to fill in the family-facing handoff (§15-03) with specific guidance for *your* gear.

## Pricing the inventory: where to look

Three sources for current market values:

1. **eBay completed/sold listings** (NOT current asking prices — only items that actually sold). Filter for "Completed Listings" or "Sold Items" on eBay. The average sold price is your reference point.
2. **QRZ.com Swapmeet** — community classifieds; prices reflect direct-sale amateur-to-amateur values.
3. **Hamfest spot prices** — what the same gear sells for at a local hamfest. Often slightly below online prices.

A useful pricing rule of thumb:

| Channel | Typical price (% of "fair market") |
|---------|------------------------------------|
| eBay sold (peak) | 100% |
| QRZ Swapmeet | 90-100% |
| Local hamfest | 70-85% |
| Dealer trade-in | 40-60% |
| Dealer consignment | 70-85% (after fees) |
| Estate buy-out (whole station) | 30-50% |

The **dealer trade-in vs. consignment** spread is significant — trade-ins are fast but pay 40-60% of value; consignment takes longer but returns 70-85%. For a high-value station, consignment usually pays better.

## The major US amateur-radio dealers

The list below covers dealers your family can reach for selling, consignment, or direct buy-outs. Verify current contact information before calling — the websites are stable; phone numbers may have changed.

### Ham Radio Outlet (HRO)

- **Website**: hamradio.com
- **Locations**: 13+ retail stores across the U.S. (CA, AZ, NV, GA, NH, NC, TX, VA, OH, etc.). Each location has its own phone number.
- **Headquarters**: Anaheim, CA.
- **Services**: New equipment sales, used equipment sales, **consignment**, **trade-ins**, mail-order, retail walk-in.
- **What they're known for**: Long-established (since the 1970s); largest amateur dealer network in the U.S.; strong consignment channel for individual radios and complete stations.

For estate disposition, HRO's consignment program is one of the best options — they handle the listing, photography, and sale, taking a percentage. Their network of stores reaches buyers nationwide.

### DX Engineering

- **Website**: dxengineering.com
- **Location**: Tallmadge, OH.
- **Phone**: (Cleveland-area area code; verify current number on website.)
- **Services**: New equipment sales (rigs, antennas, parts), trade-ins on selected equipment, RF engineering products. Less consignment-oriented than HRO.
- **What they're known for**: Comprehensive antenna and accessory inventory; trade-in program for newer rigs.

For estate disposition, DX Engineering is best for selling individual newer items via trade-in. They're not the right channel for very old gear or full estates.

### R&L Electronics

- **Website**: randl.com
- **Location**: Hamilton, OH.
- **Services**: New equipment sales, **used equipment sales**, **consignment**, repair services.
- **What they're known for**: Mid-sized regional dealer; strong used and consignment market; will buy complete stations.

R&L is a good option for full-station disposition, especially if you're east-of-Mississippi based.

### Universal Radio

- **Website**: universal-radio.com
- **Location**: Reynoldsburg, OH.
- **Services**: New + used equipment, **vintage and shortwave specialists**, repair services.
- **What they're known for**: Extensive vintage and shortwave receiver inventory; expertise on classic gear.

For vintage equipment disposition (Drake, Collins, Hammarlund, etc.), Universal Radio is one of the top options. Get an evaluation from them before selling vintage gear elsewhere.

### GigaParts

- **Website**: gigaparts.com
- **Location**: Huntsville, AL.
- **Services**: New + used amateur and shortwave gear, antenna products.
- **What they're known for**: Large catalog; growing presence in used market.

### Other channels worth knowing

- **QRZ.com Swapmeet** (qrz.com/swapmeet): the largest free-to-list amateur classifieds. Buyer pays seller directly. Family can list with help from a friend.
- **eHam Classifieds** (eham.net/classifieds): similar to QRZ; smaller but active.
- **eBay**: broadest reach, includes non-amateur buyers. **Caveat**: 13-15% seller fees, complex shipping for large items, occasional scammer issues.
- **Facebook Marketplace** + amateur-radio Facebook groups (Ham Radio Buy/Sell/Trade and similar): no fees, local pickup, but requires social-media account.
- **Local hamfest** (annual or semi-annual events): family can sell as a "tail-gate" seller for a small flat fee. Best for parts and lower-value items.
- **Local club**: many clubs help with estate sales — running an "estate sale" booth at the club's hamfest, helping price items, contacting members directly.

## Specialty / vintage dealers

For collectible gear, these specialists pay more than general dealers:

- **Universal Radio** (above) — generalist with strong vintage market.
- **Surplus Sales of Nebraska**: industrial / military surplus; some vintage radio.
- **Several private collectors and dealers** specialize in single brands (Collins, Drake, Hammarlund). Search QRZ classifieds or vintage-radio forums (boatanchor mailing list, Antique Radio Forums) to identify who's actively buying which brands.

If you have vintage gear (see §15-02), document which specific specialists exist for *your specific items* and include in the family handoff.

## Honest pricing for the family

For each major item in the inventory, compute:

- **Conservative estimate** (low end of market): what a dealer trade-in would pay = ~50% of fair market value.
- **Realistic estimate** (consignment, QRZ self-sale): ~70-85% of fair market value.
- **Optimistic estimate** (best-case eBay or specialist buyer): ~95-100% of fair market.

In the family handoff, use the **realistic** number. Note the conservative number too: "Worth $X realistically; if a dealer offers under $Y, decline."

This gives the family a defensive position against low-ball offers.

## Whole-station disposition vs. piece-by-piece

Two strategies:

### Whole-station: dealer buys everything

A single dealer (HRO, R&L, sometimes Universal) evaluates the complete station and offers a single price. Pros: fast, simple, one transaction. Cons: dealer needs margin, so the price is typically 30-50% of fair market.

Best for:
- Smaller stations.
- Family wants to be done quickly.
- No items of unusual value.
- No friends or family who want individual items.

### Piece-by-piece: sell each item separately

Each item goes to its best buyer (consignment for the IC-7610, eBay for the BaoFeng HT, Universal Radio for the Collins receiver, donation to club for parts). Pros: maximizes total realized value (often 2-3× the whole-station price). Cons: takes weeks to months; requires effort.

Best for:
- Larger stations.
- Items with significant individual value.
- Vintage / collectible items.
- Family willing or able to help (or has a knowledgeable friend).

For most amateur estates, **piece-by-piece via consignment + a few direct sales** maximizes value while keeping family effort manageable.

## Pricing matrix per inventory item

For your printable handoff, document each item with:

```
Item: IC-7610 (R-001)
Realistic resale: $2,500
Conservative (dealer trade-in): $1,400
Best channel: HRO consignment (estimate ~$2,200 after fee)
Backup channel: QRZ Swapmeet (asking $2,500; expect $2,300)
Avoid: eBay (shipping risk for heavy item)
```

This level of detail tells the family exactly how to dispose of each item without guessing.

## Selling timeline expectations

| Channel | Time from listing to cash |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Friend/family direct | 1-2 days |
| Local hamfest | 1-3 weeks (until next hamfest) |
| Local club (estate-sale help) | 2-4 weeks |
| QRZ Swapmeet (active item) | 1-3 weeks |
| eBay | 2-4 weeks |
| HRO consignment | 1-3 months (active) |
| Whole-station dealer buy-out | 1-2 weeks |

Plan accordingly. If the family wants the gear out quickly, the dealer buy-out is the fast path; if they have time, consignment is the value-maximizing path.

## Scams to flag for the family

In the family-facing section (§15-04), specifically warn about:

1. **Unsolicited dealers** who appear at the funeral or in the days after, offering "to take everything off your hands" for a small flat amount. **These are predators.** Legitimate dealers don't cold-call grieving families.
2. **Online buyers asking to wire payment** in unusual ways or who want to ship to overseas addresses. Stick to PayPal Goods & Services for online sales.
3. **"I'll come pick everything up — cash"** offers without itemized appraisal. Get an appraisal first, then sell.
4. **Anyone offering cash for the antenna tower** without seeing the tower. Tower removal is a separate (paid) service; nobody pays cash for an installed tower without inspection.
5. **Local estate-sale companies that don't specialize in radio.** They'll value the IC-7610 the same as a 1990s stereo — $50. Use radio dealers for radio gear.

## Common operator-side mistakes

- **Underestimating gear value.** "It's just a radio" — many radios appreciate (vintage) or hold value (modern HF transceivers).
- **No documentation of value.** Without a written estimate, the family will accept the first offer.
- **Listing only major items.** Tools, parts, books, software all add up; document them.
- **No backup channel.** "Sell to HRO" — what if HRO's consignment is closed for that month? List a backup.
- **Forgetting that prices change.** Annual review of estimated values keeps the document current.
