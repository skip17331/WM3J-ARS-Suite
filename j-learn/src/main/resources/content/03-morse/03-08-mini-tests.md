# Mini Tests

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> ⚙️ **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

A handful of self-administered tests you can run any week to check where you really stand. Each is short (3–10 minutes). Pick whichever matches what you want to measure today.

## Test 1 — Random character accuracy (5 min)

**Goal:** measure your raw character-recognition accuracy at the current effective speed.

**Method:** Run a 5-minute random-character session in LCWO (or any trainer) using all the characters you've learned. Set character speed to 20 WPM and effective speed to whatever you're working at. Count your errors.

**Pass:** ≥90% accuracy.

**Interpretation:** This is the standard test of "can I move up?" Pass two weeks in a row → bump effective speed by 1 WPM. Fail → drop 1 WPM and re-test next week.

## Test 2 — Common-word recognition (3 min)

**Goal:** check whether you've started hearing words instead of letters.

**Method:** Have a partner (or a trainer with a "common words" mode) send you a stream of the most common English short words at 18 WPM. Examples:

```
the and for are you all can has had was her his its one two
out got put use new old few may get let say end yes
```

Count how many you transcribe correctly. Then count how many you transcribe *as words* (writing the whole word at once) vs. *as letters* (one letter at a time).

**Pass:** ≥80% transcribed as whole words. Below that, you're still in letter-by-letter mode and should drill common words more.

## Test 3 — Callsign drill (3 min)

**Goal:** check callsign recognition specifically, which is the hardest part of CW.

**Method:** Use **RufzXP** or similar callsign-drill app. Set the start speed to 20 WPM, attempt at least 50 callsigns, let the trainer adjust the speed up and down as you go. Note the speed you sustain at 80% accuracy.

**Pass:** Sustained speed ≥ your normal random-character effective speed. (Callsigns *should* be a little harder than random text — if your callsign accuracy is much worse, drill callsigns specifically.)

**Notes:** This test is more revealing than random-character tests for predicting how you'll do in real contests and DX hunting.

## Test 4 — Send-quality test (5 min)

**Goal:** measure whether your sending is clean enough to be copied by a competent operator.

**Method:**

1. Send the following paragraph (or any 200-character text you choose) at your normal sending speed.
2. Record it (phone mic against the radio's speaker, or computer audio capture from the sidetone).
3. Play it back **a day later** (long enough that you've forgotten the source) and try to copy your own sending.

Test text:

```
the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. amateur radio
makes the world smaller. cw is a clean efficient mode for
weak signal work. tnx fer qso es 73 sk.
```

**Pass:** You can copy your own sending at ≥95% accuracy. Below that, your sending has timing irregularities that a stranger would also struggle with.

## Test 5 — On-air QSO (variable)

**Goal:** check whether your trainer skills transfer to real-world conditions.

**Method:** Find a slow CQ on 40 m or 20 m (SKCC frequencies 7.055 / 14.050 are good hunting). Answer it. Have a complete QSO. Note:

- Did you copy his callsign correctly?
- Did you copy his RST, name, QTH?
- Did you send your own info cleanly?
- Did you complete the sign-off without confusion?

**Pass:** All four yes. Bonus if you exchanged rig and weather info too.

**Notes:** This test fails for many reasons (band conditions, partner's sending, your sending) — don't take a single failed QSO as a verdict. Three failed in a row at the same speed level suggests trainer practice is overstating your real ability.

## Test 6 — 60-second sprint (1 min)

**Goal:** see how long you can hold concentration at maximum copy.

**Method:** Set a trainer to your best receive speed plus 2 WPM. Run a 60-second random-character session. Count errors only in the *first* 30 seconds vs the *last* 30 seconds.

**Pass:** Error rate roughly constant across the minute. If errors spike in the second half, your concentration is the bottleneck, not your decoding speed — you can copy fast for a few seconds but tire quickly.

**Notes:** Important for contesting, where holding pace for hours matters. Practice longer sessions if this test reveals concentration limits.

## Test 7 — Word-count rate (5 min)

**Goal:** estimate your real word-per-minute throughput, including writing-down time.

**Method:** Run a 5-minute session of plain English text (not random characters) at your effective speed. Transcribe it. Count the words you got right.

```
Words right / 5 minutes = real-world WPM throughput
```

For a 15 WPM trainer effective speed, you should be transcribing around 12–13 real WPM, not the full 15 — the difference is your writing-down time and minor errors.

**Pass:** Real throughput ≥80% of trainer effective speed.

**Notes:** This is the number that actually matters in operating. Trainer speeds are convenient, but real QSOs are limited by real throughput.

## Test 8 — Headcopy test (5 min)

**Goal:** check whether you can copy without writing — the highest level of CW skill.

**Method:** Same as Test 7, but **don't write anything**. At the end of 5 minutes, write down what you remember of the content.

**Pass:** You can summarize the gist of what was sent. Recovering specific phrases is bonus.

**Notes:** Very advanced — most operators don't reach this stage for years. If you can pass this at 18 WPM, you're well ahead of the average ham. Don't bother with this test until your written-copy speed is solid at 18+ WPM.

## How often to test

| Test | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Random character (Test 1) | Weekly — your main progress metric |
| Common-word (Test 2) | Monthly — check word recognition |
| Callsign drill (Test 3) | Monthly — especially before contests |
| Send-quality (Test 4) | Monthly — your sending degrades without notice |
| On-air QSO (Test 5) | Weekly if active; less if not |
| 60-second sprint (Test 6) | Quarterly — check stamina |
| Word-count rate (Test 7) | Quarterly — sanity-check your trainer numbers |
| Headcopy (Test 8) | Yearly, if interested |

## Recording results

A simple spreadsheet or text file. The trend matters more than any single number.

```
Date          T1     T2    T3    T4    T5    T7    Notes
2026-02-01    9/90%  -     -     -     -     -     Bumped from 8
2026-03-01    10/92% 75%   12W   95%   pass  9 W   First on-air QSO
2026-04-01    12/91% 82%   14W   95%   pass  10 W
2026-05-01    13/90% 85%   15W   97%   pass  11 W
```

Watch the trends. Real progress moves the numbers slowly but consistently. Plateaus are normal. Backsliding for a week or two is normal too. Long-term, the line points up.
