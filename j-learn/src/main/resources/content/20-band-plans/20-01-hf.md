# HF

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> ⚙️ **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

This section is the band-by-band reference for amateur HF in the United States. Each band entry covers the **legal allocations** (per FCC 47 CFR §97.301 / §97.305), the **license-class sub-bands** (who can operate where), and the **voluntary mode conventions** (CW/digital/SSB segments).

These numbers are correct as of the current FCC rules; the FCC adjusts amateur allocations from time to time, so verify against the current 47 CFR text if precision matters (e.g., before submitting a complaint).

## Quick reference: all US HF bands

| Band | Frequency range | Bandwidth |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| 160 m | 1.800–2.000 MHz | 200 kHz |
| 80 m | 3.500–4.000 MHz | 500 kHz |
| 60 m | 5.330.5, 5.346.5, 5.357.0, 5.371.5, 5.403.5 MHz (5 channels, USB only) | n/a (channelized) |
| 40 m | 7.000–7.300 MHz | 300 kHz |
| 30 m | 10.100–10.150 MHz | 50 kHz |
| 20 m | 14.000–14.350 MHz | 350 kHz |

| 12 m | 24.890–24.990 MHz | 100 kHz |
| 10 m | 28.000–29.700 MHz | 1700 kHz |

## US license-class restrictions

The FCC allocates portions of each band by license class. The general rule:

- **Technician**: very limited HF (essentially 10 m phone and small CW segments on 80/40/15/10 m).
- **General**: most HF, with some Extra-only segments excluded.
- **Advanced** (no longer issued, but existing licensees retain their privileges): between General and Extra.
- **Extra**: full access to all HF amateur frequencies authorized to amateur radio.

The detailed sub-bands by class follow.

## 160 m (1.800–2.000 MHz)

The "top band." Long-distance propagation under quiet ionospheric conditions; a winter-night DX band.

| License class | Modes |
|---------------|-------|
| General, Advanced, Extra | All authorized modes 1.800–2.000 MHz |
| Technician | No HF privileges on 160 m |

There is no FCC sub-band allocation by mode within 160 m for Generals/Extras — all modes are allowed across the band. Power restrictions apply: 1500 W PEP from 1.800-1.900, with notes on lower-power restrictions in some specific frequencies (per §97.313).

### Voluntary 160 m mode plan

| Frequency | Convention |
|-----------|-----------|
| 1.800-1.810 | CW QRP / weak signal |
| 1.810-1.830 | CW |
| 1.830-1.840 | CW DX window |
| 1.840-1.843 | FT8 (de facto standard) |
| 1.843-1.999 | SSB (LSB) — DX window 1.830-1.835; nets/contests |
| 1.999-2.000 | Beacons (rare on 160 m) |

160 m is mostly CW and digital below 1.840; mostly SSB above. The 1.840 transition is conventional, not legal.

## 60 m (5.330.5–5.405.0 MHz, 5 channels)

Unique among amateur bands: **only 5 specific channels are allowed**, all USB only, all secondary status (must yield to primary users — military, public safety).

| Channel | Center frequency (carrier suppressed) | USB display |
|---------|--------------------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | 5.330.5 MHz | 5.332 MHz |
| 2 | 5.346.5 MHz | 5.348 MHz |
| 3 | 5.357.0 MHz | 5.358.5 MHz |
| 4 | 5.371.5 MHz | 5.373 MHz |
| 5 | 5.403.5 MHz | 5.405 MHz |

The "carrier suppressed" frequency is the historical channel center; the USB display is the radio's center-of-passband indicator. Many radios let you key in either; verify your rig is putting RF in the legal channel before transmitting.

| License class | Privileges |
|---------------|-----------|
| Technician | None |
| General, Advanced, Extra | All 5 channels, USB only, 100 W ERP max |

### Voluntary 60 m mode plan

USB voice on all 5 channels. CW and digital are allowed (subject to ERP and bandwidth limits) but mostly under-used.

## Common HF DX windows

These are the conventional "DX-only" windows where serious DX operators expect not to find domestic chatter:

| Band | DX CW window | DX SSB window |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| 160 m | 1.830-1.835 | 1.840-1.845 (LSB) |
| 80 m | 3.500-3.510 | 3.790-3.800 (LSB) |
| 40 m | 7.000-7.025 | 7.155-7.180 |
| 20 m | 14.000-14.020 | 14.180-14.200 |
| 17 m | 18.068-18.080 | 18.140-18.160 |
| 15 m | 21.000-21.020 | 21.300-21.330 |
| 12 m | 24.890-24.910 | 24.940-24.960 |
| 10 m | 28.000-28.020 | 28.700-28.800 |

These are voluntary; many countries' DX-peditions operate split (transmitting in their home segment, listening in a different segment).

## Common mistakes

- **Operating SSB on 30 m.** It's CW/digital only — federally restricted. Don't.
- **Phone in a Tech segment using a Tech callsign on a band you don't have privileges for.** Verify your privileges before transmitting.
- **Calling CQ on 14.074.** That's the FT8 frequency — a CQ in SSB or CW there is interference to a heavily-used digital channel.
- **Tail-ending in a DX window.** The DX-pedition is operating; calling CQ in their window pre-empts everyone trying to work them.
- **Treating a contest as a license to ignore the band plan.** Contests have their own conventions; some run wider in the SSB segments. The band plan still applies.
