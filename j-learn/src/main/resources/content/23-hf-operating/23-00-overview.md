# HF Operating Techniques — Overview

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

This chapter is about *technique* on HF — the tactical decisions that turn a licensed operator into a working DXer, contester, or grayline chaser. It assumes you already know the etiquette (the unwritten rules that keep the bands usable) and want to know how to actually *get the contact* — the rare island, the all-band sweep, the JA opening at sunrise.

The previous chapter ([§22 Operating Practice](../22-operating-practice/22-00-overview.md)) was the etiquette manual: how to share the bands, identify, run a pile-up without being a nuisance. This chapter is the next layer up: now that you can operate cleanly, how do you operate *effectively*?

The two are different skills. Plenty of polite operators never break 50 DXCC entities; plenty of effective operators are also lids. The goal is to be both.

## The big idea

HF operating is a *skill stack*. The bottom of the stack is regulation (you must identify, you must stay in your privileges); the next layer is etiquette (you must listen, you must use minimum power); the top is technique (you must read the band, time your call, pick the right frequency). Each layer assumes the ones beneath it.

A few principles thread through the chapter:

- **The band is alive.** Propagation changes minute by minute. The skill is reading what's open now, not what was open yesterday. Cluster spots, panadapter, the WSPR network, and your own ears — together they tell you where to operate.
- **Timing matters more than power.** A 100 W station that calls at the right moment beats a 1500 W station that doesn't. The DX's "QRZ" is a 1–2 second window; missing it costs you the contact.
- **DXing rewards patience and preparation.** Knowing the DXpedition's operating plan, having your VFOs set up before they start, and being patient when they're working another region — these win contacts that brute-force calling won't.
- **Contesting is a different sport.** Same radios, same bands, but contesting is rate-driven: 30–60 contacts an hour is the floor, not the ceiling. The exchange is minimal; the focus is throughput.
- **Mobile and portable HF have their own constraints.** A loaded whip on a car has a 5–10% efficient antenna; a wire in a tree is 50–90% efficient. Knowing what's possible from each setup shapes your operating choices.
