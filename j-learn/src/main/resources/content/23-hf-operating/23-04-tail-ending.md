# Tail-ending

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

## What it is

**Tail-ending** is throwing your callsign in *just before* a station finishes a QSO and calls QRZ. You catch the back-end of the previous contact and signal that you're next in line — without waiting through the full "QRZ" cycle.

Done right, it's efficient and even appreciated by the station you're calling. Done wrong, it's interrupting and rude. The line between them is timing and the kind of contact in progress.

## The convention

A standard QSO ends with each side saying 73 and the station running the run saying "QRZ." Listen carefully — the *exact* end of the running station's transmission is the tail. Right at that moment, before the next "QRZ," another caller's voice appears with their full callsign:

```
DX:    "...thanks 73 K1ABC, Q-R-Z"
                              ↑
                       Tail-end caller drops in here:
You:   "Whiskey Mike Three Juliet"
DX:    "WM3J 5-9, QRZ"
```

You're slipping in during the brief silence between "K1ABC" and "Q-R-Z" (or right on top of "QRZ" itself). The running station picks you because you were ready when others weren't.

On CW the timing is the same:

```
DX:    "K1ABC TU 73 QRZ"
You:                "WM3J"
DX:    "WM3J 5NN"
```

## When it's OK

Tail-ending is fine — even expected — when:

- **The DX is running fast.** A station working 60 QSOs/hour expects tail-enders. Waiting through every full QRZ slows the whole rate.
- **It's a contest.** Contest cadence is so fast that tail-ending is the norm. No explicit QRZ, no pause — just the running station's call and the next exchange.
- **The DX is operating simplex** (not split). In split, tail-ending happens on the listen frequency just like every other call.
- **The QSO that's ending is a routine pile-up exchange** — signal report, thanks, 73, QRZ. The next caller (you) doesn't disrupt the rhythm.
- **You can be brief and accurate.** Drop your call once, clearly. No second call, no "calling DX," just the call.

## When it's rude

Tail-ending becomes a problem when:

- **The ongoing QSO is a ragchew, not a pile-up.** If two operators are clearly having a conversation (talking about antennas, weather, rigs), they're not "running" — they're chatting. Don't interrupt.
- **You jump in during the *middle* of a transmission.** The tail is the *end*. If the station is still saying "5-9 thanks for the contact," you're inside their transmission, not at the tail.
- **You call before the running station's signal report is acknowledged by the other side.** The current QSO isn't complete until both stations have confirmed the exchange. Calling before that point means you're disrupting an incomplete contact.
- **You tail-end someone calling CQ on a clear frequency.** They're trying to start a QSO, not finish one. Just call normally.
- **You tail-end into a contest exchange that's still going.** Wait for "QRZ" — even contesters need the half-second after the previous exchange.

## A worked timing example

Here's the same situation done well and done badly:

**Done well (tail-end at the right moment):**

```
DX:    "...K1ABC 5-9 thanks 73, QRZ Whiskey Mike Three Juliet QSL?"
                                 ↑
                       You drop your call here:
You:   "Whiskey Mike Three Juliet"
DX:    "WM3J 5-9, QRZ"
```

You waited until "K1ABC" (the previous caller's confirmation) was complete and the DX had begun "QRZ." Clean, fast, efficient.

**Done badly (premature tail-end):**

```
DX:    "K1ABC 5-9 thanks—"
                          ↑
                You drop your call here, on top of "73":
You:   "WHISKEY MIKE THREE—"
DX:    "—73 sorry, QRZ"
```

You interrupted the running station's own transmission. The DX hears your call as garbage, hears their own audio as garbage, and the previous QSO with K1ABC is now confused. Bad.

The difference is a half-second. Listen for the previous caller's signal report being acknowledged — that's your cue.

## The "wait for QRZ" question

A traditional view says: never tail-end, always wait for QRZ. A modern view says: tail-ending is fine when the DX is running. Both are defensible.

In practice, the running DX station decides. They'll either:

- **Pick up your tail-end and work you immediately** — they wanted the rate, and you delivered it.
- **Say "next time" and explicitly say "QRZ"** to slow you down — they prefer the formal call-and-response.
- **Ignore your tail-end and respond to a different caller** — they're picking the cleanest copy, not the fastest.

If the DX consistently says "QRZ" before taking any calls, that's their signal: wait. If the DX takes tail-end callers without comment, you can tail-end.

## Tail-ending in contests

Contest cadence makes tail-ending the default. The exchange is so short that the running station expects each QSO to overlap slightly:

```
You:  "CQ Contest WM3J"
K1A:  "K1A"
You:  "K1A 5-9 PA"
K1A:  "5-9 MA"
You:  "QRZ WM3J"
K2B:           "K2B"           ← K2B tail-ends, dropping their call almost on top of "WM3J"
You:  "K2B 5-9 PA"
K2B:  "5-9 NJ"
You:  "QRZ WM3J"
```

K2B's tail-end is appropriate — they're calling immediately on the running station's QRZ, taking advantage of the next slot. In a 100-QSO/hour run that's just the cadence.

## On the receiving side

If you're the one calling CQ and running, decide your tail-ending policy and stick to it:

- **Accept tail-enders.** You'll get more contacts. Risk: occasional confusion if two tail-enders overlap.
- **Reject tail-enders.** Slower, but every QSO is clean. Say "QRZ" deliberately and pause briefly before accepting calls.

Either is fine. Most fast-rate operators accept tail-ends; most contest operators do too. Casual operators often prefer the formal cadence.
