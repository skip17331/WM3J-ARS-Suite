# POTA — Parks On The Air

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> ⚙️ **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

## What it is

**Parks On The Air (POTA)** is amateur-radio operation from designated parks — national parks, state parks, forests, monuments, historic sites, and recreation areas. Activators set up a portable station inside a park, make ten or more contacts, and earn the park as "activated." Chasers contact activators to earn the park toward their own awards.

POTA emerged in 2017 as a successor to the National Parks On The Air (NPOTA) centennial event run by the ARRL in 2016. It went global, opened up far more parks than just US national parks, and dropped the time-limited centennial structure. Today POTA is one of the largest and most active portable amateur-radio activities in the world.

## How POTA differs from SOTA

POTA and SOTA share DNA but differ practically:

|  | POTA | SOTA |
|--|------|------|
| Where | Any qualifying park | Mountain summits only |
| Access | Usually drive-up (parking lots) | Hike-up, often strenuous |
| Activation threshold | 10 QSOs | 4 QSOs |
| Points system | Award-based, not point-accumulation | Point-accumulation toward "Mountain Goat" |
| Database | pota.app | sotadata.org.uk |
| Equipment | Can be large/heavy (drive in) | Must be backpacked |
| Weather flexibility | Stay in the car if needed | Exposed to elements |
| Growth since 2020 | Explosive — millions of QSOs | Steady |

POTA is **dramatically easier** than SOTA. A casual day in a state park with a folding antenna and a 100W rig is a typical POTA activation. SOTA demands a serious hike.

## Park references

Every POTA-eligible park has a reference in the format `K-NNNN` for US parks (other countries use country prefixes). Examples:

- `K-0027` — Acadia National Park, Maine
- `K-2253` — Letchworth State Park, New York
- `K-4239` — Walden Pond State Reservation, Massachusetts
- `EA-0001` — Spain, Picos de Europa National Park

The `pota.app` website maintains the complete park list and the activation/chaser leaderboards.

To qualify as a POTA park, the location must:
- Be a designated park, forest, monument, recreation area, etc.
- Be on the official POTA list (curated by region admins)
- Allow public access (some parks require permits, which is the operator's responsibility)

## Equipment for POTA

Because most POTA parks are drive-up, you can bring substantially more gear than for SOTA. A typical setup:

| Item | Typical product |
|------|----------------|
| HF rig | Anything portable — Icom IC-705, Yaesu FT-891, Xiegu G90, Elecraft KX2 |
| Power | Car 12 V system, LiFePO4 20–50 Ah battery, or a small generator |
| Antenna | End-fed half-wave on a fiberglass mast, dipole between trees, hamstick on a tripod, or a screwdriver vertical |
| Logging | Phone or laptop (HamRS app, N3FJP POTA Logger, or paper) |
| Comfort | Camp chair, folding table, sunshade, weather radio |

Equipment weight is irrelevant for park activations; comfort and convenience win.

## Operating practice

A POTA activation:

1. **Drive to the park.** Check the park's entrance hours and any radio-specific rules (some parks require a permit for organized activity).
2. **Set up.** Find a clear spot — picnic table, parking area, or a trail crossing. Deploy the antenna. A 32 ft EFHW for 40/20/15 is the universal choice.
3. **Self-spot on pota.app.** This is essential — chasers reload the spotting page constantly looking for new activations.
4. **Call CQ:** `"CQ POTA, CQ POTA, this is Whiskey Mike Three Juliet portable, Letchworth State Park K-twenty-two-fifty-three, calling CQ POTA."`
5. **Run 10–40 contacts** in 30 minutes to a few hours. Most QSOs are 20–40 seconds.
6. **Log every contact** with time, call, mode, RST, the chaser's location (state/country/park if doing P2P).
7. **Submit your log** to pota.app via the website (ADIF upload). Park activations credit within hours.

## Park-to-park (P2P) QSOs

A special joy of POTA: another activator in another park calls you. This is a **park-to-park (P2P)** QSO and counts for BOTH operators as an activation contact. P2P contacts are doubly valuable and add up fast on busy weekends.

The exchange: each operator gives their park reference. `WM3J K-2253` works `W1ABC K-2017` and both log a P2P.

## Common operating frequencies

POTA operates on the same HF voice and CW segments as general operating, with self-spotting being the key discovery mechanism. Popular bands by time of day:

- **Morning (0700–1100 local):** 40 m (7.180–7.200 MHz SSB, 7.030 CW)
- **Midday (1100–1500 local):** 20 m (14.295 MHz SSB, 14.060 CW)
- **Afternoon/evening (1500–2000):** 17 m and 40 m
- **Night (2000+):** 80 m for regional traffic

POTA activators broadcast their TX frequency in the spot, so chasers don't need to tune.

## Awards and the leaderboard

POTA has many awards based on accumulated park count and contact count:

- **Activator awards:** Activate 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 / 250 / 500+ unique parks.
- **Chaser awards:** Contact 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 / 250 / 500+ unique parks.
- **Worked All Parks** (WAP) awards for completing a state or region.
- **Park-to-Park** specific awards.
- **Special events** (Field Day weekends, holiday events) often double as park weekends.

The leaderboards on `pota.app` are public, fueling friendly competition.

## Etiquette

- **Self-spot.** No spot, no chaser traffic.
- **Listen first.** Some parks see multiple simultaneous activators. Pick a clear frequency 5–10 kHz away from any other POTA activity.
- **Be brief.** A 30-second QSO is normal. Don't ragchew with the first chaser when 40 others are waiting.
- **Confirm park reference** in every exchange.
- **Respect the park.** Don't damage flora, don't disturb wildlife, follow leave-no-trace, follow posted park rules.
- **Don't activate without a permit if one is required.** Some parks (especially national parks with special-use rules) require advance authorization for organized radio activity.

## Why POTA exploded post-2020

Three factors made POTA the runaway success:

1. **Pandemic-era outdoor activity boom.** Lockdowns drove a million people into parks; amateur radio came along.
2. **pota.app is well-designed.** The interface is fast, mobile-friendly, and the self-spotting system removes friction.
3. **Self-spotting + cellular ubiquity.** You can be in a park 10 miles from anywhere and still spot yourself on the network. SOTA shares this, but POTA's lower bar made it accessible to more operators.

In 2020, POTA had ~150,000 contacts. In 2024 it was over 8 million. The community is now mature and the activity level on weekends rivals contest activity.
