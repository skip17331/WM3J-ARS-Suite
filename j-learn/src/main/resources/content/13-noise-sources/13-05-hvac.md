# HVAC

> **Advanced callout convention:** sections or paragraphs intended for Extra-class / engineering depth are marked with a blockquote starting `> ⚙️ **Advanced —**`. Simple-mode renderers can hide these; advanced-mode renderers show them inline.

Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems include several components that can produce RFI: motor controllers, smart thermostats, electronic ignition modules. Most are quiet at full operation but can be loud during startup or in degraded condition.

## Common HVAC noise sources

| Component | Noise type | When it's loud |
|-----------|------------|----------------|
| Furnace blower motor | 60 Hz hum, motor commutation hash | When blower is on |
| AC compressor motor | Inductive transient at startup | Brief — at compressor start/stop |
| Heat pump variable-speed driver | Switching hash | Whenever heat pump is operating |
| Electronic ignition module | Brief high-voltage arc | Briefly at gas furnace ignition |
| Smart thermostat | Wi-Fi/Zigbee bursts | Periodic; sync intervals |
| Variable-speed blower (ECM) | Continuous PWM hash | Whenever blower runs |

The common thread: HVAC equipment cycles. The noise comes and goes with the cycle, which makes it both annoying and easy to identify.

## Identifying HVAC as the source

The pattern is distinctive:

- **Noise correlates with the heating/cooling cycle.** If you hear hash for 10 minutes, then silence for 30, then hash again — that's HVAC.
- **The thermostat has a "fan on" mode.** If turning that mode on adds noise, the blower motor is the source.
- **Air conditioning during summer = added noise during peak operation.** Heating during winter = same.

To confirm: turn off the HVAC at the breaker for 10 minutes. Does the noise stop entirely? If so, HVAC is responsible (at least for that portion of the noise floor).

## Specific fixes by component

### Furnace blower motor (commutator type, older furnaces)

Old shaded-pole or capacitor-start motors with brushes generate brush noise — sparking at the commutator surface. Cures:

- **Replace worn brushes.** A licensed HVAC technician can do this in 30 minutes; brushes cost $20.
- **Add a snap-on ferrite** to the motor's power leads at the motor itself.
- **Replace the motor with an ECM** (electronically commutated motor — quieter electrically, but introduces PWM hash; tradeoff).

### Variable-speed (ECM) blower

The modern alternative; quieter mechanically but adds PWM hash on the AC mains. Cures:

- **Add common-mode chokes** on the motor's power leads.
- **Add chokes at the air handler's mains entry.**
- **Confirm proper grounding** — bond the air handler's chassis to building ground via a short wire.

These are often installed without proper RFI consideration; chokes after the fact help significantly.

### AC compressor

Inductive load that produces voltage transients at startup and shutdown. Most modern compressors include a "soft start" capacitor that limits the transient.

Cures:
- **Add a soft-start kit** (HyPer Engineering Easy Start, Micro-Air, etc.) to older compressors. Cost $200–400, installation by HVAC tech. Reduces noise AND extends compressor life.
- **Snap-on ferrite** on the compressor's power leads at the air handler.

### Electronic ignition module (gas furnace)

Brief high-voltage arc each ignition cycle. Cures:

- **Replace a deteriorating module.** If you hear the arc on the radio every time the furnace lights, the spark gap may be eroding.
- **Add ferrite to the ignition module's wiring.**

### Smart thermostat

Wi-Fi and Zigbee/Z-Wave radios in the thermostat. Cures:

- **Add ferrite to the thermostat wires** (the low-voltage cable from the thermostat to the air handler).
- **Move the thermostat away from the antenna feedline route.**
- **Disable Wi-Fi if not needed** — most smart thermostats can run in offline mode.

## Diagnosis tips

### Sound character

Different HVAC components have characteristic sounds:

- **Brush-type motor**: harsh, scratchy hash that varies with motor speed.
- **ECM motor**: smooth PWM hash, often whining at a higher pitch.
- **Compressor startup**: brief low-frequency thump at start/stop.
- **Ignition arc**: brief, sharp click at furnace ignition.
- **Thermostat radio**: brief bursts at intervals.

### Time correlation

Run the radio with a recorder. Note when noise starts and stops. Match against the HVAC cycle (most thermostats can show a cycle log). The cause is whatever runs at the times the noise appears.

### Spectrum width

A narrow tonal noise is usually digital (thermostat, controller). A broad hash is usually motor or switching converter. The spectrum analyzer of an SDR (§12-08) makes this obvious.

## When HVAC is the household priority

Sometimes the household needs the HVAC running and you can't operate during HVAC cycles. Practical:

- Operate during HVAC-quiet periods (the system is off when the house has reached setpoint).
- Add receive-antenna nulls aimed at the HVAC equipment (a Beverage receive antenna oriented away from the air handler).
- Operate higher bands (HVAC noise is mostly low-band).

## When the HVAC is in a critical area

Some HVAC equipment is so old or so noisy that there's no good fix without replacing it:

- A 30-year-old gas furnace with a deteriorating ignition module may need to be replaced anyway for efficiency reasons.
- An old AC compressor with bad starting capacitors is often near end-of-life.

If your HVAC is approaching replacement age, ask about RFI when shopping. Some heat pump brands (Mitsubishi, Trane) are quieter; some (cheap commercial units) are very noisy.
